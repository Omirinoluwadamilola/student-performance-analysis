# Student Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

This project analyzes academic and behavioral data from 1,000 students to identify patterns associated with final examination performance.

The analysis examines study time, attendance, previous grades, internet access, parental education, extracurricular activities, part-time employment, and other student characteristics.

The objective is to transform student-level data into actionable insights that can support better educational decision-making and targeted academic interventions.

## Objective

The primary objective of this project is to identify factors associated with student academic performance and use data analysis to generate meaningful recommendations for improving educational outcomes.

## Tools Used

## Key Questions

* What is the overall distribution of student performance?
* Which factors are most strongly associated with final exam scores?
* How does attendance vary across performance groups?
* Is study time associated with academic performance?
* How do internet access and part-time employment relate to average scores?
* Which students may benefit from additional academic support?

## Key Findings

### Study Time

Study time showed the strongest positive relationship with final exam performance among the main numerical factors analyzed, with a correlation of approximately **0.57**.

### Previous Academic Performance

Previous grade also showed a positive relationship with final exam performance, with a correlation of approximately **0.41**.

### Attendance

Average attendance increased across grade categories, from approximately **77% for F-grade students to 88% for A-grade students**.

### Internet Access

Students with internet access recorded an average final score of approximately **84.3**, compared with **79.3** for students without internet access.

### Part-Time Employment

Students without part-time jobs recorded an average final score of approximately **84.6**, compared with **81.3** for students with part-time jobs.

### Overall Performance

**63.8%** of students achieved an A or B, while **10.1%** received a D or F.

## Recommendations

1. Use attendance, previous performance, and study patterns to identify students who may need early academic intervention.
2. Encourage structured study habits and provide academic support resources.
3. Monitor consistently low attendance and intervene before performance declines.
4. Explore ways to improve students' access to digital learning resources.
5. Provide flexible academic support for students balancing school with part-time employment.
